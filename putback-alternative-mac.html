Putback alternative on a Mac: what differs

18 November 2026 · Martín Acuña Lledó · Applies to macOS 13 Ventura through macOS 27

Putback and Many Displays solve the same daily problem: you come back to your Mac and the windows are piled on one screen. Both remember where windows were and put them back when the displays return, without a layout to save. If you are choosing between them, or using Putback and wondering what else is out there, this page compares what each one says it does. I make Many Displays, so read that part with that in mind. I have not tested Putback for this page. Everything about it comes from putback.app, checked on 18 November 2026, and can change.

What Putback says it does

  • Memory, not tiling. Its site calls it a window manager built on memory instead of tiling. It snapshots your desk as you go, with no setup or rules, and keeps a separate layout for every display setup.
  • Restore on return. When your displays come back, it says every window slides back to its place, and that layouts survive docks, hubs and sleep. It quotes under a second for a typical full-desk restore.
  • Scenes. Named desks you can summon with a hotkey, drawn in its Scene Studio, including apps that are not open yet. Its FAQ says Scenes can also apply on a schedule or when a video call starts.
  • Spaces. Its FAQ says it works within the Space you can see on each display, never records or moves windows on your other Spaces, and that full multi-Space memory is on the roadmap.
  • Requirements and price. macOS 14 Sonoma or later, a universal build for Apple silicon and Intel. $19 one time for 1 Mac, $29 for 5 Macs, $79 for 20 Macs, with a 7-day trial and no account. The $19 tier says updates are included. It is signed and notarized, and its site says layouts never leave your Mac and it has no trackers.
  • Not covered on its site. It does not describe how it handles two identical monitors, and says Sidecar and DisplayLink screens have not been tested yet.

Fixes without an app

Whichever app you pick, these cost nothing and reduce how often anything has to be put back. The full list is in every fix for windows moving after sleep.

  • Keep the displays connected while locked. In System Settings → Lock Screen, set "Turn display off when inactive" to Never and use a password-protected screen saver, and turn off deep sleep in each monitor's menu. This covers locks, not real sleep or a dock.
  • Turn off "Displays have separate Spaces" in System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Mission Control, so sleep changes a window's display but not its desktop. Putback's FAQ describes a different combination that helps macOS's own memory, so try both and keep what works for you.
  • Give identical monitors their own ports. On Apple silicon the physical port is what can tell them apart. See two identical monitors swap places after sleep.

Where the two differ

  • Other desktops. Putback says it does not record or move windows on Spaces you cannot see. Many Displays remembers windows on every desktop and restores them without switching to them. A few apps hide their off-Space windows, and those are restored when you visit their desktop. Neither can move a window to a different Space: since macOS 14.5 no app can without disabling System Integrity Protection.
  • Identical monitors. Putback's site does not say how it tells them apart. Many Displays uses the system UUID, then the physical port on Apple silicon, then the EDID manufacture date, and only then position.
  • Display settings. Many Displays also puts back the arrangement, main display, resolution, refresh rate, rotation and HDR if macOS changed them, and turns off mirroring macOS turned on after a reconnect, before it moves windows. Putback's site does not mention restoring display settings.
  • Named layouts. Putback has Scenes, with hotkeys, triggers and a visual editor. Many Displays has none.
  • macOS. Putback needs macOS 14. Many Displays runs on macOS 13 or later.
  • Licences. Both are $19 one time. Putback: 1 Mac, with updates included per its $19 tier, and 5 Macs for $29. Many Displays: one person on up to 3 Macs, one year of updates, the last version of that year yours to keep, and a 14-day trial plus a 14-day refund.
  • Source. Many Displays' source is on GitHub. Putback's site does not say it is open.

Four questions to choose by

  1. Do you use several desktops per monitor? If yes, check how each app handles windows on desktops you are not looking at.
  2. Are your monitors identical? Check whether the app says how it tells them apart, and test a few sleeps.
  3. Do you switch between set arrangements during the day? If yes, Scenes are a real feature that Many Displays does not have.
  4. Which Mac and macOS? Putback needs macOS 14. On macOS 13, only one of the two will run.

How Many Displays handles it

Many Displays saves within about 1.5 seconds of a window moving, and freezes saving the moment the Mac locks, sleeps or a display disconnects, so the pile macOS makes is never recorded. It keeps one layout per combination of displays. When the Mac wakes or unlocks, or the monitors come back, it waits until the displays have been stable for a few seconds, puts back the display settings, and then returns every window to its display, position and size. A setup it has never seen starts from the closest one it knows. For three minutes after a restore, changes nobody asked for are undone instead of saved.

Where it may not suit you:

  • There are no named layouts or Scenes to switch between. If you want a hotkey for "presentation mode", Putback does that.
  • On the MacBook alone it goes idle and does not move windows.
  • It does not reopen windows you closed, and does not launch apps for you.
  • Two identical monitors from the same batch behind one dock can match on serial, port and date, and then only position is left.

It needs macOS 13 or later and the Accessibility permission. If you try it while Putback is installed, turn off Putback's automatic restore or quit it, so two apps are not moving the same windows.

Questions

What is the main difference between Putback and Many Displays?

Both remember where your windows are and put them back when your displays return, with no layout to save. Putback adds Scenes, named desks you can summon with a hotkey. Many Displays has no named layouts, and instead puts back display settings such as arrangement, resolution and rotation, tells identical monitors apart by port and manufacture date, and covers windows on your other desktops.

Does Putback restore windows on other Spaces?

Not today. Putback's FAQ says it works within the Space you can see on each display and never records or moves windows on your other Spaces, and that full multi-Space memory is on its roadmap.

Which is cheaper?

Both start at $19 one time. Putback's $19 licence covers one Mac, its $29 Family licence covers five, and its site says updates are included. Many Displays' $19 licence covers one person on up to 3 Macs, with one year of updates, and the last version of that year stays yours.

Can I try both?

Yes. Putback has a 7-day trial and Many Displays a 14-day trial, neither needs an account. Run only one at a time, so two apps are not moving the same windows after a wake.
